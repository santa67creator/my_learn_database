# Lecture 8. Random variables, their types. Law of distribution

**Random variable** is a quantity that, as a result of testing, will take one, previously unknown value, depending on random reasons. Denoted by $X, Y, Z$; possible values ​​are $x, y, z$.

- **Discrete SV** - takes individual, isolated values ​​(countable number).
- **Continuous SV** - completely fills a certain gap (infinite number of values).

## Law of distribution

Correspondence between possible values and their probabilities. Tabular form (distribution series):

| $X$ | $x_1$ | $x_2$ | $\ldots$ | $x_n$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P$ | $p_1$ | $p_2$ | $\ldots$ | $p_n$ |

Normalization condition: $p_1+p_2+\ldots+p_n = 1$.

Graphically represented by a **distribution polygon** - points $(x_i, p_i)$ connected by segments.

> [!example] Example - lottery
> 100 tickets: one winning 500 tenge, ten winning 100 tenge. $X$ — the cost of winnings for the owner of one ticket.
>
> | $X$ | 500 | 100 | 0 |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $P$ | 0.01 | 0.1 | 0.89 |
