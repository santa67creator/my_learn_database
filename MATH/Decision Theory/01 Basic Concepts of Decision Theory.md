# Lecture 1. Basic concepts of decision theory

**Decision Theory (DMT)** is the science of the principles and methods of optimal choice in all areas of purposeful activity.

- **Goal-oriented activity** is an action based on the desire to achieve a specific goal.
- **DM** (decision maker) - an individual or group who makes a decision and is responsible for it.

According to Max Weber, there are four types of social actions: **target-rational** (conscious goal), **value-rational** (belief in the intrinsic value of an action), **traditional** (out of habit), **affective** (under the influence of emotions).

## Stages of the decision-making process
Description of the goal → description of possible courses of action → building a mathematical model → collecting data → setting the problem → finding a solution → interpretation → implementation and control → correction.

## TPR, operations research, systems analysis
- **Operations Research** is a branch of applied mathematics that builds mathematical models for analyzing control and decision-making problems.
- **System analysis** - study of the problem of decision making in a complex system.
- Key concepts of system analysis: **element**, **connection**, **system**, **system structure**, **decomposition**, **hierarchy** (tree and diamond).

## Normative vs descriptive approach
- **Normative approach** - rational, based on the concept of maximum utility: the decision maker has a utility function, the optimal solution is with its maximum.
- **Descriptive approach** - based on the concept of **bounded rationality** (G. Simon): the decision maker is not looking for an optimal, but a satisfactory solution, simplifying reality.

## Decision making strategies
- **Multi-criteria strategies**, **compensation strategies** (additive strategy, additive difference strategy, ideal point strategy) - bad assessments on some parameters are compensated by good ones on others.
- **Exclusion strategies** - discard unpromising alternatives without calculating the overall score: **dominance** (an alternative is no worse than others in all parameters, better in at least one), **conjunctive** (minimum requirements for all parameters at once), **disjunctive** (score based on the best parameter), **lexicographic** (first according to the most important parameter, then according to the next one), **removal by aspect**.
