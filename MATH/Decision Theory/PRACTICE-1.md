## 1. Theoretical questions

**What is a decision?** This is a conscious choice of one course of action out of several possible ones to achieve a goal.

**What is the decision-making process?** This is a sequence of actions: awareness of the problem → search for options → determination of criteria → evaluation of options → selection → implementation and verification of the result.

**Who is a decision maker?** A decision maker, that is, a person (or group) who chooses an option and is responsible for the consequences.

**What are the possible solutions?** Solutions are:

- rational and intuitive;
- programmable (standard) and non-programmable (new, non-standard);
- individual and group;
- taken under conditions of certainty, risk or uncertainty.

**What influences the choice of decision?** The choice is influenced by the goals and values ​​of the decision maker, available information, time, resources (money, effort), risk, experience, emotions, the opinions of others and cognitive distortions.

**What is the difference between a good solution and a bad one?** A good solution:

- decided on the basis of sufficient information and clear criteria;
- justified and takes into account risks;
- brings you closer to the goal.

A bad decision is made impulsively or under pressure, without analysis and ignoring the consequences. It is important that a good decision does not always give a good result (it is influenced by chance), but it is made in the right way.

## 1.1. Job A or Job B

**How will the student make a decision?** He will identify the problem (choosing a job), compare options based on criteria that are important to him, evaluate the pros and cons, and choose the option that best suits his goals.

**What factors will he take into account?**

- salary amount (250,000 versus 350,000 tenge, difference 100,000 tenge);
- distance to home (time and money for travel);
- workload and schedule (fatigue, time to study, rest, personal life);
- health and emotional state;
- development prospects and experience;
- opinions of family and friends;
- personal goals (to save money or maintain a balance).

It is important that part of the difference in salary will be “eaten up” by travel and fatigue, so the real benefit of work B is less than it seems.

## 2. Practical task No. 1: “Disassemble the solution”

**Problem.** A university graduate must choose a place of work from three options, which differ in salary, distance and workload, as well as growth opportunities.

**DM.** A graduate himself. Parents and friends can give advice, but the choice and responsibility for it remain with him.

**Alternatives.**

- A: 250,000 tenge, near the house, light load.
- B: 350,000 tenge, far from home, high load.
- B: 280,000 tenge, average workload, good career opportunities.

**Selection criteria** (4 or more):

1. Salary amount.
2. Distance to home (travel time and costs).
3. Load level and schedule.
4. Career opportunities.
5. Work-life balance, health.
6. Gaining experience and skills.

**Goal.** Choose a job that will provide a decent income, preserve health and time to live, and create the basis for long-term development.

**Option rating** (on a scale of 1 to 5, with 5 being best):

|Criterion|A|B|C|
|---|---|---|---|
|Salary|2|5|3|
|Proximity to home|5|1|3|
|Load|5|1|3|
|Career growth|1|3|5|
|Experience and skills|2|4|5|
|**Total**|**15**|**14**|**19**|

**Selected solution: option B.**

**Why.** The salary of 280,000 tenge is lower than in B, but the difference of 70,000 tenge is partially offset by lower travel costs and a lower risk of burnout. Option A is comfortable, but does not provide development, and in 2–3 years the student may find himself at the same point. Option B only wins in terms of money, and high workload and long travel reduce the quality of life and can lead to burnout. Option B provides balance, because today’s small shortfall in income will be compensated by an increase in position and salary in the future.

If the goal were different, for example, money was urgently needed (loan, help for family), it would be more reasonable to choose B. The decision depends on goals and values.

## 3. “Normative or descriptive approach?”

**Situation A: normative approach.** The student compared the cost of training, distance, prospects and future salary and chose the option with the maximum expected benefit. He acts as a rational person “should” act: there are criteria, analysis and utility maximization.

**Situation B: descriptive approach.** The university was chosen only because a friend studies there. This is social influence and emotional attachment, and there is no analysis. This is how people often make decisions in real life.

**Situation B: descriptive approach.** A person chose an expensive phone, although there was a cheaper analogue with the same characteristics. Here, brand prestige, status, habit and the “famous brand” effect were at work, rather than rational benefit.

**Conclusion.** The normative approach describes the ideal, and the descriptive approach reflects the actual behavior of people, which is influenced by emotions, environment and limited information.

## 4. “Types of behavior”

|№|Behaviour|What influenced the decision|
|---|---|---|
|1|Takes a long time to analyze and compare according to criteria|**Rationality**: the desire to make an informed decision. Disadvantage: time consuming, risk of analysis paralysis.|
|2|Decides immediately, based on first impression|**Emotions and intuition**, impulsiveness, possibly lack of time or experience. The solution is quick, but risky.|
|3|Consults with friends and parents|**Opinions of others**, self-doubt, lack of information and experience, desire to reduce risk.|
|4|Postpones until the last minute|**Fear of mistakes and responsibility**, anxiety, avoidance of choice, sometimes lack of information.|

In reality, the factors are usually mixed: for example, a person can both analyze and consult.

## 5. Case “The Headman’s Choice”

**DM.** A group of students who vote. If the choice is made by the curator or the dean's office, then he. In the case scenario, the group decides.

**Alternatives.** Aliya, Daniyar, Madina. Additionally, you can consider the options of “rotation of prefects” or “choose two (prefect and deputy).”

**Criteria.**

- responsibility and reliability;
- ability to communicate with teachers;
- ability to communicate with a group and organizational skills;
- activity;
- diligence (carrying out instructions);
- punctuality.

**Rating:**

|Criterion|Aliya|Daniyar|Madina|
|---|---|---|---|
|Responsibility|+|− (forgets)|+|
|Contact with teachers|−|±|+|
|Contact with the group|±|+|±|
|Organizational skills|±|+|±|
|Executiveness|+|−|+|

Madina has no weaknesses in terms of the key criteria for a head teacher (responsibility, connection with teachers, diligence), and her weak point (low activity) is less critical. Therefore, it is the most reliable choice. Daniyar is suitable if the group values ​​​​cohesion and activity.

**Goal.** Select a leader who will ensure effective communication between the group and teachers and organized work of the group.

**Factors influencing the decision:**

- personal sympathies and friendship;
- opinions of friends and group leaders;
- past experience of communicating with candidates;
- the importance of certain qualities for a person;
- group pressure, desire to be “like everyone else”;
- limited information about candidates.

**Why do different people make different decisions given the same information?** People have different values ​​and priorities: for some, responsibility is more important, for others, sociability. People assess risk differently, they have different experiences, emotions and relationships with candidates, and they also weigh criteria differently (they give different “weights” to the same data). Therefore, the same set of facts can lead to different conclusions. This is what explains the need for decision theory: it offers methods that make choices more reasonable and transparent (for example, weighted evaluations of criteria).

## 6. “Real Life Decision Making”

Below is an example on the topic “buying a phone.” Replace it with your actual situation if necessary: ​​the structure will remain the same.

1. **Problem:** Your old phone often freezes, runs out of battery quickly, and you need to buy a new one within a limited budget.
2. **DM.** I myself (sometimes with the advice of parents or friends).
3. **Possible options.**
    - an expensive flagship of a famous brand;
    - a mid-range smartphone with good characteristics;
    - budget model;
    - a used phone of the previous generation.
4. **Selection criteria.** Price, performance, camera quality, battery capacity, reliability and update support period, warranty.
5. **Factors influencing the decision.**
    - budget;
    - reviews and reviews;
    - advice from friends;
    - fashion and brand prestige;
    - advertising and discounts;
    - personal experience in using technology.
6. **Selected option.** Mid-range smartphone.
7. **Why was he chosen?** In terms of price/quality ratio, he best met the criteria. The flagship is much more expensive, and I would hardly use its advantages, and I would mainly pay for the brand. The budget model is weaker in performance and battery, and the used option is riskier in terms of condition and warranty. The middle class provides sufficient speed, a good camera and a guarantee at a reasonable price.
